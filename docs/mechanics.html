TOSSA

How the game works — every rule, every number. Revised 29 July 2026.

The three pillars. Everything below serves these, and anything that would break one is not in the game.
  • Skill first. Stats govern access and comfort — which props and counts are practical, how forgiving a catch is. Player skill governs excellence: score, records, high counts. No stat ever plays the timing for you.
  • Juggling is real. At least one prop must be in the air for a catch to score. Throwing one object up and down is not juggling and pays nothing.
  • The first impression is calm. A warm theatrical stage, chill music. The excitement is in catches, streaks, tricks and unlocks — never in flash.

1   The throw

A throw is one continuous gesture. Tapping the throw key gives the lowest, snappiest throw; holding it lifts the throw continuously toward the ceiling. Height buys airtime and costs accuracy.

Big loads must fly higher simply to have room to weave. From five objects the tap floor scales: ×1.30 at five, and +0.15 for every object above that, so fifteen objects tap at ×2.80 the airtime of three.

2   The catch

A catch is judged purely on timing: how many ticks your press falls from the prop's true arrival. Where the prop is on screen never matters — only when.

PERFECT±7 ticks always secured. Scores 100 × streak × multipliers, and continues the streak.

LATEto +28 ticks a poorer chance. Scores a flat 10 × multipliers, and resets the streak.

Perfect always works. Good and Late are a probability:

chance = base + (Accuracy/100) × 0.30 − height × 0.35 + prop + gear + Strength

3   The four stats

Points come from levelling: 3 + level/12 per level. All four caps together cost 850 points, which arrives at about level 115 — roughly 30–40 hours of skilled play. Every piece of content has unlocked long before that, by level 20–25.

Accuracy — cap 250

RangeEffect
1 – 100Lands throws closer to their mark: inaccuracy scales from ×1.00 at Accuracy 1 down to ×0.35 at 100. Also raises the off-Perfect catch chance by up to +0.30.
101 – 200 FOCUSEvery point past 100 is Focus. The first 100 Focus cut stamina costs, from ×1.00 down to ×0.25.
101 – 250Focus also widens the Perfect window: +1 tick per 10 Focus, capped at +6, and never past the Good window of 10. This is the one place a stat touches timing, and it is deliberately small and hard-capped.
Skill walls: Columns at 50, Half Shower at 150.

Strength — cap 200

Strength is the access stat. It gates how many objects you can field and which props you can throw at all. Every gate is one even step of 16.7 points.

Objects456789101112131415
Strength needed1633506683100116133150166183200

Throwing a prop you are under-strength for is punished on three axes at once: the tap floor sags by up to 40%, the extra height a hold buys is halved, and inaccuracy roughly doubles — with a floor, so even a low under-strength throw stays unsteady. Being over a prop's requirement lofts it a little higher and steadies it in the hand.

Endurance — cap 200

Maximum stamina is 100 + 9 × Endurance, so a capped juggler carries 1,900. Stamina persists between acts through the day; sleep and meals restore it. Below 25% of maximum, fatigue starts dragging effective Accuracy down, reaching ×0.50 at empty. At empty your hands stop closing and the act collapses.

Speed — cap 200

Speed slows the whole world, which stretches every catch window in real time without changing the simulation. Every 8 points gives 1% of slow motion, so the cap is 25%. Slow motion sources stack:

A Purist act keeps the two earned bursts and strips the two bought ones.

5   Patterns

Exactly one pattern is running at any moment. P switches between the ones you have, live, without restarting. Each has its own score multiplier.

PatternPaysThe shape
Cascade×1.00The weave. An odd count crosses to the other hand; an even count keeps each hand its own circle. Every throw leaves the inside and lands on the outside.
Columns×1.15Every object goes straight up and comes down where it left, in fixed lanes. An odd count opens a middle lane too. Lanes sit between 44 px and 100 px from the centre line and never spread wider, however many objects — so the stance never widens.
Half Shower×1.25The dominant hand throws over the top; the other keeps the weave but low — capped at 0.65 of a tap, so the two roads stay clearly apart at every count.
Shower×1.40The full circle, one direction. The dominant hand makes every high throw (floor 0.55) and the other returns a fast flat pass: 0.16 of a tap, at a fifth of the usual rise. Dealt one-handed — everything in the throwing hand, one in the other — and switching to it re-deals.
Reverse Cascade×1.45Both hands throw over the top. The weave runs backward.
Tennis×1.50One marked prop sails over the top, back and forth across the weave, 20% higher than an ordinary over-the-top throw because it crosses a whole pattern rather than one road.

6   Tricks

Tricks are separate from patterns and ride on top of whichever one is running. C cycles which trick is loaded; the trick key uses it.

TrickKindWhat it doesBonus
Behind the Backtoggle Every throw loops behind the back while it runs. Switch on, switch off.60
High Tosstoggle Every throw flies sky high — a tap already reaches 1.35, and holding takes it to the 1.80 ceiling, high enough to leave the frame for a beat.90
Spinlive You turn on the spot for 44 ticks. Every catch during a Spin scores double, and you catch blind: the off-Perfect chance drops by 0.28. 96-tick cooldown, and it needs at least one prop airborne.—

Only one toggle runs at a time — switching replaces the running one. A Spin can run at the same time as a toggle. A trick bonus is paid on a clean catch of the throw it shaped.

Which tricks go with which patterns

A pattern and a toggle can run together, and a Spin can run on top of either. Fifteen of the eighteen combinations are clean. Three are not — not because they are hard, but because the two shapes contradict each other — and the game warns you as you switch them on rather than refusing. Measured as mid-air clearance at five clubs with realistic catch timing:

Patternalone+ High Toss+ Behind the Back
Cascade+29+19+7
Columns+29+29−14
Half Shower+28+38+17
Shower+19−4+3
Reverse Cascade+24+21−10
Tennis−0.4+4+15

A negative number means the props can touch in flight. Behind the Back under Columns is the clearest contradiction: a column is defined by coming down where it left, and Behind the Back by releasing from across the body — both cannot hold. Behind the Back under Reverse Cascade puts two crossings on one road. High Toss under a Shower crowds a high road that already carries every prop. Tennis's own −0.4 is not a combination problem: that pattern flies an over-the-top road straight through the weave it crosses, and always has.

One continuous performance at a chosen venue, with three goals. From the first goal on you may cash in at any time: press the finish key, or catch every prop and hold them — the empty air ends the act.

Every completed act at a venue raises that venue's bar by a further 15%, permanently. The World Arena is where the game is won: set a record there. The Midnight Show is bonus content that opens afterwards.

How an act ends

  • Cash in. Every point on the board is kept. Available from the first goal.
  • Collapse. The crowd empties, three drops are made, or stamina runs out. The score is stripped back to the last goal reached — and to nothing if no goal was.
  • Stranded. Down to one prop and holding it: the act finishes itself, because a single object can never score again.
  • Standing ovation. Not one prop dropped, no collapse, at least one goal banked: the room stands, flowers rain down, and the night pays +35% on top.

The crowd

The crowd's patience only ever falls unless it is fed. Perfect catches, big throws and Spins feed it; Late catches and whiffs drain it. Where the meter sits decides what the room does:

ApplauseWhat happens
above 0.78 GREENthey throw flowers, on their own beat — every 26 ticks, one roll per thrower
0.34 – 0.78with you, but nothing comes up
below 0.30 REDthey throw tomatoes instead. Worth nothing at the counter, but eatable later for +14 stamina
emptythe room walks out and the act collapses

What they throw depends on both how well you are playing and how grand the house is: a flower is worth 1, a posy 5, a gold rose 25, a bouquet 60. The rare tiers only appear deep in the green with drops at or near zero — and only a big house sends a bouquet at all. The night's pay is simply what the crowd threw, so it was earned in front of you.

10   Reputation, pay and records

  • Reputation swings by up to 60 on how many of the three goals an act reached. Reaching none of them loses reputation. Reputation is the key to the venues, and nothing else.
  • Pay is what the crowd threw, multiplied by 1.4 on a Purist act. A broke juggler always earns at least a busking floor, so the road never ends purely because a night went badly.
  • Records are an act's score, per venue, on two separate boards: an ordinary one and a Purist one. A Purist act runs with no stats, no gear and no bought slow motion — only your hands — and pays and scores 40% more for it.
  • Mastery. Once all four stats are capped, each level-up converts its points to money instead, at 250 apiece.

12   How skills are earned

Tricks and patterns are dealt, not chosen. Clearing a wall — a level, a stat, a stage, a run of acts performed — earns a card, face down. Turning it over reveals one skill you do not already have, with every remaining one equally likely. Until then a locked skill shows nothing about itself but its kind.

Cascade is yours from the start and never enters the deck. If six acts pass with no unlock at all, the game deals a card anyway.
