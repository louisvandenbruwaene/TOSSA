# JUGGLE: Story Treatment

Version 0.1. A companion to GDD.md. This works out the "light story" the GDD
promises (Section 1) into a concrete arc, cast, and a venue-by-venue climb. It
is a proposal, not locked. Names in particular are placeholders you can change.

The guiding rule: the story should dramatize the game's core pillar, not decorate
it. The pillar is that stats buy you a living but skill makes you great (GDD 4.1).
So the story is about exactly that argument, made human.

---

## 1. Premise and theme

A young juggler with more talent than money climbs from a backyard to the world
stage. Every rung tempts them to take the easy path: buy better props, grind the
numbers, dazzle with gear instead of craft. The story keeps asking whether the
climb is bought or earned, and the ending answers it on the biggest stage there
is, where only real hands can win.

One line: talent is cheap, mastery is earned, and the crowd always knows the
difference.

---

## 2. Cast

**Milo** (placeholder name, renameable by the player). The protagonist. Broke,
stubborn, and genuinely gifted. Starts with three worn balls and nothing else.
Milo is the player, so Milo says little. Their character comes through choices
and through how the crowd reacts, not through speeches.

**Bruna**, the teacher. An old street juggler past her performing days, sharp and
warm, who gives Milo the first lesson in a backyard. Bruna is the voice of the
pillar. Her refrain, roughly: gear and muscle only carry you to the door, the
crowd is what you earn once you are inside. She reappears at the milestones as a
letter, a note left backstage, a face in a crowd.

**Silvio Grand**, the rival. A showman a few years ahead of Milo on the same
ladder. Silvio has the best props money can buy, maxed numbers, and a manager. He
is not a villain, he is the tempting shortcut made flesh. He wins early rounds on
polish and spectacle. He drops under real pressure because his timing was never
tested, only his equipment. Silvio is what Milo could become by chasing stats
instead of skill.

**The crowd**, treated as a character. It is fickle, honest, and unbribable. The
Applause meter is its voice. It cannot be bought, only moved. Every venue has its
own crowd personality (see below), which is the real difficulty curve of the
story wearing different clothes.

---

## 3. Shape of the arc

Three acts across ten venues (the GDD Section ladder).

- **Act one, the door (venues 1 to 3).** Learn the craft, earn the first coins,
  discover that the crowd pays for feeling, not for showing off. Ends when Milo
  can hold a real cascade in front of strangers and get paid for it.
- **Act two, the temptation (venues 4 to 7).** The ladder gets expensive.
  Heavier props, real competition, and Silvio. Milo can now afford shortcuts, and
  the game quietly lets them try. The act is about learning that a bought
  advantage tops out, and that the residual gap at the top is all skill.
- **Act three, the proof (venues 8 to 10).** Endurance, records, and the finale.
  No shortcut survives here. The systems that mitigated difficulty lower down are
  stripped or maxed, and what remains is timing. Milo either has it or does not.

---

## 4. The ten venues

Each stage lists its setting, its story beat, the crowd it teaches you to read,
and what it introduces mechanically, so the story and the systems unlock in step.

**1. The Practice Room (backyard).**
Bruna teaches the first throw. No money, no crowd, no stakes. This is the
tutorial: three balls, catch and throw, the no-crossing hands. The story beat is
small and human, an old juggler handing a kid three balls and saying start.
Introduces: balls, catch and throw, POISE. Cutscene: the first lesson.

**2. The Street Corner.**
Milo busks for the first time. A hat on the ground, passersby who do not stop
unless the juggling is clean. First gold, first Reputation, first taste of the
crowd as judge. Introduces: performing versus practising, money, Reputation,
the Applause meter. Cutscene: the first coin in the hat.

**3. Local Festivals.**
A weekend fair, a friendlier crowd that rewards a little flair. Milo can now
afford rings and learns that a new prop is a wall before it is a skill. Introduces:
rings (Accuracy investment), higher ball counts, the first trick slot. Crowd
lesson: warmth, they want to like you, give them a reason.

**4. The Small Theatre.**
Indoors, ticketed, a quieter and more exacting crowd. Clubs arrive here, and with
them real weight. Silvio Grand headlines the bill above Milo and is gracious in
the way that stings. Introduces: clubs (Strength), the shop matters now, tricks as
loadout. Crowd lesson: restraint, a theatre crowd punishes flailing. Cutscene:
buying the first club.

**5. The Circus.**
Milo joins a travelling troupe. Backstage life, a found family, and knives. The
troupe is where Milo could start buying the shortcut, matching Silvio prop for
prop. The game lets them. It also shows the ceiling of doing so. Introduces:
knives (Endurance and an Accuracy safety floor), longer sets, prop variety in one
routine. Crowd lesson: spectacle, but spectacle that is dropped is worse than
none. Cutscene: joining the circus.

**6. The National Championship.**
A scored competition, not a show. Here the score screen, not the hat, is the
judge. Silvio wins on paper with maxed stats and the priciest torches, and Milo
places second, close, on a cleaner run with worse gear. The lesson lands: the gap
that stats close is real but it is small, and it is closing from Milo's side.
Introduces: torches (the top prop), competition scoring, the first Purist board.
Crowd lesson: precision over flash. Cutscene: learning torches.

**7. The International Championship.**
The best in the world, and the first stage where bought advantage plainly is not
enough. Silvio, out of his depth against true masters, cracks under a long set and
drops. Milo, poorer but tempered, holds. The rivalry inverts here. Introduces:
the highest counts, stat mitigation stripped on the ranked boards, the taming
curve at its steepest (GDD 4.8). Crowd lesson: they have seen everything, only
mastery reads as new.

**8. The World Record Attempt.**
Not a performance, an ordeal. A single very long routine where Stamina and
Endurance are the whole test and one drop ends it. This is the Focus payoff stage,
where trained Accuracy past 100 is what lets a human sustain the impossible.
Introduces: the endurance format, Stamina and Focus as the binding constraint.
Cutscene: the record attempt.

**9. The World Arena.**
The spectacle stage. Full slow-motion reward moments, the biggest crowd, every
prop. If slow-mo ships, this is where it sings. The story beat is triumph earned,
Milo as a genuine headliner, Silvio now in the audience. Crowd lesson: give them
the thing only you can do.

**10. The Legendary Finale.**
The boss. Five torches, a vast crowd, a long set with advanced requirements and
many Perfect catches (GDD Final Challenge). No mitigation, no gear that helps,
only hands. Win and earn the title, The World's Greatest Juggler. Bruna is in the
front row. Cutscene: the legendary final performance.

---

## 5. Silvio's parallel arc

Silvio is the control group for the experiment the game is running. He goes up the
same ladder ahead of Milo, always with the better numbers and the better props. He
peaks at the National Championship, where money still buys enough of an edge to
win. He fails at the International, where it does not. His last appearance is in
the arena crowd, watching Milo do the thing he never learned to do. He is not
punished by the story, he is simply overtaken, which is the point. The shortcut is
not evil, it just runs out.

---

## 6. How the ending pays off the theme

The finale strips every mitigation the meta ever offered. Reputation does not
help you catch. Glasses cannot save a mistimed hand. Focus only widens the window
a few frames, the rest is you. So the last thing the player does in the whole game
is the first thing Bruna taught them, a clean catch and a clean throw, done under
the most pressure the game can bring. The credits should feel like the backyard,
closed into a full circle.

---

## 7. Tone and delivery

Warm, wry, unhurried. This is a life on the road, not a melodrama. Story is
delivered lightly: short illustrated cutscenes at the eight milestones (GDD
Cutscenes list), a handful of Bruna's notes, and the crowd doing most of the
emotional work in real time. No dialogue trees, no exposition dumps. The player
should be able to skip every cutscene and still feel the arc through the climb
itself, because the climb is the story.

Writing style for any on-screen text: plain, warm, few words. The crowd cheers and
groans. Bruna is terse. Milo is silent. Let the juggling talk.

## Update 2026-07-10: the arc with teeth

Louis asked for a story that grips, not a fellow making a living with fancy
quotes. The rewrite in cutscene_script gives the climb a wound and a debt.

Bruna was the Marvel of Two Hands, the best alive, until the gear circuit
learned her hands could not be bought and every door in the country closed at
once. The three worn balls she presses on Milo are all she kept of her act.
Milo's climb is therefore an argument: every clean catch says she was right
forty years ago. Silvio Grand is the gear empire's heir, and money beating
hands is the one thing his life cannot afford to see disproved, which is why
his barbs sting and why his fall at the internationals is quiet rather than
funny. When his gear throws a torch a hand's width wrong and he stares at his
own empty hands, the lie stops holding him. The record ordeal is Milo counting
one clean catch for every year they took from her, onto pages the gear never
printed, because the record they buried was hers. The finale seats her in the
front row under her own name again, Silvio sends two tickets and a note that
reads one debt paid, and in the last shot she returns the balls in the yard
where it started. She was keeping them for whoever came to finish it.
